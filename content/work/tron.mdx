## The problem

Nobody asked for brand guidelines. I joined TRON DAO as a contract brand designer to help evolve the identity, and right as I started, a miscommunication between the US and China teams led the CN team to launch a micro-rebrand. The result was a piecemeal system that was hard to scale, and the whole US brand operation depended on one internal designer. Rather than wait it out, I used the moment to give the brand real structure.

I wrote and designed the guidelines, from the audit to the rollout. Justin Yee, the lead US brand designer, gave insight along the way and helped stress test the rules while we built the guidelines site.

## The audit

Justin and I pulled every asset, social post, deck and visual reference from both regions into one FigJam board. Side by side, the problem was clear. Individual graphics looked great, but the visual language had no shared rules. I took the board to leadership. With them, the case wasn't about taste. It was about speed: every inconsistency was a question someone had to stop and ask the one designer who knew the answer. We agreed on a direction that kept the momentum of the recent update while adding the structure it was missing.

## Nine categories

I distilled the guidelines into nine categories. The first, Brand Foundation & Strategy, sets the narrative pillars that every piece of work, from summit graphics to developer posts, anchors back to. The rest turn those pillars into rules a designer, vendor or agency can follow without asking.

## When to scale, and when not to

Not everything should come out of a template. The Brand Scale puts every kind of asset on a line from functional to expressive. Decks, docs and data cards sit at the functional end, where anyone can build them from templates and get them right. Campaign key art and hero social sit at the expressive end, where the work stays one of a kind and comes back through design.

## A living guidelines site

A PDF goes out of date the moment it's exported, so I built the guidelines as an internal Figma Sites hub that updates in real time. Building the site was also how we stress tested the rules, with Justin pushing on each page as it went up. Once leadership and legal signed off, I turned the rules into componentized Figma libraries: evergreen templates, assets and UI elements ready to use.

## The outcome

The brand went from a fragmented, one-designer bottleneck to an organized system that scales. US TRON marketing teams in events, partnerships, operations and PR all work from the new guidelines, without waiting on design. A simplified, external-facing version gives brand partners and agencies the same rules without the internal detail.
