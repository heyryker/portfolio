Some of my favorite projects are big launches. Some are small rectangles that show up in your LinkedIn feed. This post is about the rectangles.

## Video ads

From storyboards to working with motion designers and content writers, video ads have become one of my favorite ways to tell a story. A good one has to land the idea before someone scrolls past, so every frame needs a job.

## Display ads

B2B marketing runs on paid media, and paid media runs on sets. One idea has to work as a skinny skyscraper, a wide leaderboard, a square and a LinkedIn post, without falling apart at any of them.

<Figure src="/blog/lucid-ads/fedramp-light.webp" width={1616} height={1448} narrow alt="A set of Lucid display ads in light gray and blue announcing “Lucid is now FedRAMP Authorized,” in leaderboard, rectangle, skyscraper and half-page sizes, each with a small diagram and a Learn more button." caption="The FedRAMP launch set." />

The FedRAMP set had a simple job: tell government buyers Lucid was now authorized. The headline does all the talking, and a small diagram with collaborator avatars keeps it from feeling like a press release. I made a dark version too, for placements where light ads would disappear.

## Template thumbnail redesign

For years, Lucid’s template thumbnails were straight screenshots of the templates. At thumbnail size, that meant a gallery full of tiny, cluttered, unreadable diagrams.

I redesigned the thumbnails for our most popular templates and set the art direction for hundreds more, then handed the system to an outside agency to produce at scale. Each one boils its template down to the shape you’d recognize: a few boxes for an org chart, colored columns for a backlog, bars for a Gantt chart. They share one palette and one line weight, so the whole gallery reads as a set.
